# LIVE-5 live retrieval diagnostic

This document is diagnostic evidence, not an execution plan and not a
publish-ready acceptance. Current routing and implementation truth live in
`docs/current/`. LIVE-5 is an owner packaging label, not a PLAN-ID.

## Scope and safety

The run used the canonical `python -m ai_youtube create` path. Five network
classes were approved by name and no others: `semantic_brief`,
`provider_search`, `asset_download`, `preview_download`, `voice_preflight`.
Paid actions were limited to the two the owner authorized — semantic brief
through the existing OpenAI contract, and one ElevenLabs narration pass.
Vision stayed off, Envato and every paid stock provider stayed off, and no
second paid voice generation was attempted.

The standing paid semantic policy was enabled temporarily
(`maximum_calls_per_project: 12`, `maximum_budget_usd: 1`, model
`gpt-5.4-mini`), and `assets.use_local_library` was set to `false`. Both files
were restored byte-for-byte after the run and verified by SHA-256 against
pre-run copies; `git status` is clean on `config/`.

One deviation from the prompt's literal command, made for correctness, not
convenience: Git Bash on this host corrupts non-ASCII `argv`, which was proven
before spending anything (the Cyrillic source text arrived as 345 characters
instead of 338). The run therefore called the same canonical entrypoint
`src.ai_youtube.cli.main.main` with a programmatically built argument list, so
the source text reached the pipeline byte-exact (338 characters, 9 newlines,
identical to the 14.08 baseline input). No flag and no behaviour was changed.

Project: `projects/2026-08-15_solnechnaya-panel-lovit-svet-tolko-dnem-nochyu-2`.
The `-2` suffix is the pipeline's own collision suffix: a free `--dry-run`
preflight had already claimed the unsuffixed id. The real run created a fresh
project and did not resume over it.

## Result

The run completed end to end: `draft_completed`, all stages `completed` except
`quality_check`, which is `needs_review`. Unlike the 14.08 baseline it did not
stop at the paid voice gate.

All 5 scenes received a slot — 7 slots in total, since scene_004 and scene_005
are composite. `missing_scenes` is empty. Every slot is a real provider asset:
no `generated_fallback`, no emergency card, no local-library asset was selected.
Zero provider errors across 234 attempts returning 1303 results.

Media coverage is `image_only_draft_fallback`: 7 image slots, **0 video slots**,
`video_duration_ratio` 0.0, `publish_ready` false. Video candidates existed and
one was even downloaded (`scene_003_pixabay_12543.mp4`); ranking preferred
images. Completion tiers: 4 × `C_good_context`, 2 × `B_composite`,
1 × `D_partial`; `scenes_publish_ready` is 0 of 5.

Artifacts produced: `assets_manifest.json`, `missing_assets.json`, the review
board (`assets/review/visual_review_board.html` plus its manifest),
`narration.wav` with five scene MP3s, `draft_1080x1920.mp4` (5.0 MB, 23.83 s
narration), `quality_report.json`, and the persisted usage counters.

**Subtitles are not in the MP4.** `draft_1080x1920.mp4` and
`draft_no_subtitles.mp4` are byte-identical (same SHA-256), and the quality
report says "Subtitles are missing." The deliverable is narration over stills,
without burned-in subtitles.

## Request budget and the named defects

Hard ceiling `max_provider_requests_per_scene: 64` was respected: per-scene
attempts were 16, 62, 59, 33, 64. scene_005 landed exactly on the ceiling.

- **`C82` — reproduced.** scene_005 reached exactly 64 requests, and the string
  `request_budget_exhausted` appears **zero** times anywhere in the manifest.
  The stop is invisible in the scene-level ledger, exactly as the row predicts.
  Scene-level `provider_attempts` lists also under-report: they hold 15/20/15/15/20
  entries against the manifest-level 16/62/59/33/64.
- **`C79` — reproduced, and it is the most expensive defect in this run.** Every
  scene carries untranslated Russian in the matchable fields: scene_001
  `should_include: ["Солнечная","днём"]`, scene_003 `["быстрее","дешевле",...]`,
  scene_005 `["полдень","закате","outdoor solar farm"]`. Provider metadata is
  English, so those terms can never match — the meaning-bearing words are
  present but inert. scene_005 is the clearest case: the scene turns on
  *sunset, not noon*, and both words survive only in Russian. Provider queries
  themselves were clean (all 220 dispatched queries were `en`); the leakage is
  in the semantic record used for scoring.
- **`C75`/`C76` — reproduced, unambiguously.** Wikimedia video: **44 attempts,
  0 results**, every single one. Wikimedia images returned 99 over the same 44
  attempts. This is the predicted outcome of dropping Commons video by mime
  and/or filtering media type after a five-item fetch.
- **`C77` — consistent, not isolated.** Internet Archive returned 233 results
  over 44 attempts with a 30% zero rate, and its candidate pool drifted exactly
  as the row describes: for solar queries it surfaced "(Walfas/Prop) Vanilla
  Daylight Detector Slab", "BUILDING THE B-24 BOMBER DURING WWII" and
  "Wallpaper Images (1,362 Total)". No IA candidate was selected. The run does
  not isolate query form from ranking, so this is corroboration, not proof.
- **`C78` — reproduced as a measurable gradient.** Zero-result attempts carried
  systematically longer queries for the tolerant-word-order providers and not
  for stock: Internet Archive 6.3 words when empty vs 3.6 when not, NASA 5.6 vs
  2.8, Wikimedia 4.8 vs 3.3. Pexels and Pixabay never returned zero for a
  non-empty query. One string, five different tolerances.

Incidental observation, recorded and not fixed: 14 attempts were dispatched with
an **empty query string** (`status` and `result_count` unset), spending budget on
nothing.

## Two new defects found by this run

Both are recorded as registry rows against existing owners, neither is repaired
here.

- **`C83` — `use_local_library` is a dead config key.** The owner disabled the
  local library for this run by setting `assets.use_local_library: false`. That
  key is referenced **nowhere in the code** — only in the channel config file.
  `rank_local_assets` is called unconditionally at
  `src/news/asset_manifest_builder.py:389`. Consequence in this run: local
  candidates entered scoring for scene_003 and scene_005 (scored 7.5 and 15.0,
  all rejected below threshold). They made 0 provider attempts, never appeared
  in per-scene `ordered_providers`, and won 0 slots, so the stock-retrieval
  measurement stands — but the isolation the owner asked for was nominal, not
  real. This trips the prompt's stop condition "локальная медиатека всё же
  участвовала в отборе"; it was discovered after the run, in the artifacts.
  It also produced the run's sharpest single finding: the local library holds
  **"Автоматизированная линия сборки солнечных панелей"** — an automated solar
  panel assembly line, exactly what scene_003 needed. It scored **7.5** and was
  rejected as `score_below_60`, while the Opel car dashboard scored **72.9** and
  won. The correct material was present and lost to a wrong one, because the
  local titles are Russian and matching is English-token based. That is `C79`
  and `C83` compounding.
- **`C84` — the semantic-brief call ceiling is per adapter, not per project.**
  `build_semantic_brief_adapter()` is called fresh inside
  `src/news/visual_plan.py:75`, and the counter lives on the adapter instance
  (`self.calls`, `src/content/semantic_brief_openai.py:219,229`). Each plan
  build therefore gets its own full allowance of `maximum_calls_per_project`.
  Draft completion accumulates the *reported* spend across replans and says so
  explicitly ("Carry prior spend forward while keeping each adapter's call cap
  unchanged", `src/news/draft_completion.py:326`), which is why the persisted
  total is an honest 15 against a stated ceiling of 12. The accounting is
  correct; the ceiling is misnamed. This sharpens the existing
  "Semantic-brief live limitation" note, which asserts `maximum_calls_per_project`
  is the hard runtime bound.

## Verdict

**PARTIAL.**

Against the owner's thresholds: 5 of 5 scenes have a usable/reviewable slot and
rights are clean, which clears the coverage and rights halves of PASS — but only
2 of 5 selections are right by meaning, short of the 4 of 5 that PASS requires
and far from STRONG PASS. It is not FAIL: coverage improved over the 3/5
baseline, no scene lacks usable material, and no rights contract was broken.

One qualification the owner should decide, because it is theirs to decide: if
the 12-call ceiling is read as a hard project contract, the run's 15 calls
breached it and the owner's own rule would make that FAIL. I record it as a
misnamed ceiling (`C84`) rather than a breach of intent, because the $1 budget
the ceiling was standing in for held at $0.15.

Comparison with the STOCK repeat of 14.08, axis by axis:

| Axis | Baseline 14.08 | LIVE-5 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scenes with a slot | 3/5 | 5/5 | better |
| Scenes right by meaning | not scored | 2/5 | new measurement |
| Video slots | 0 | 0 | unchanged |
| Unique assets | 3/3 | 7/7 | unchanged (still no duplicates) |
| Rights | blocked (2 scenes empty) | verified 7/7 | better |
| Search attempts / results | 125 / 678 | 234 / 1303 | more search |
| Provider errors | 0 | 0 | unchanged |
| Endpoint | stopped at voice gate | draft MP4 + WAV | better |

This is one data point. It measures that coverage, rights and end-to-end
completion improved, and that selection quality is the binding constraint: the
pipeline now reliably fills every scene, and fills three of five with the wrong
thing. Nothing here shows retrieval is "improved" in general, and the owner's
standing note that more runs are needed is unchanged.
